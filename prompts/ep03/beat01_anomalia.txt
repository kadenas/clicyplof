Vertical hyper-realistic macro claymation video for a preschool show. A seamless pure white studio: white floor, plain white back wall, no horizon line, soft even studio lighting with gentle contact shadows. Smooth 24fps motion, not choppy.

Two small figures made of the same blue modelling clay, toddler-like: big round heads, two tiny black dot eyes each, a small simple smiling mouth, no clothes. They have very different silhouettes. Character A is tall and thin: a long narrow body like a stretched sausage of clay, long thin legs and long thin arms, much taller than the other one. Character B is short and round: a fat ball-shaped body almost as wide as it is tall, very short stubby legs and arms, about half the height of A.

Standing on the floor in the right third of the frame, from the very first frame, there is a tree made of modelling clay: a thick brown clay trunk and, on top of it, one big round green clay canopy, a solid ball of green clay with no holes and no gaps. The tree is about twice as tall as A. The characters play with a big red clay ball, about the size of their heads. Nothing in the scene is blue except the two figures.

Everything in the frame is made of real modelling clay (plasticine), and everything behaves like real clay: matte surface, visible fingerprints and thumb marks, soft rounded edges, slightly uneven hand-made shapes. Nothing is rigid, shiny or plastic.

The clay is soft and springy, and it deforms on every contact:
- When a character lands, jumps or falls, its body squashes flat on impact and then springs back up, wobbling like a jelly before settling.
- When a character walks or runs, its body stretches upward on the way up and squashes down on each step.
- When something heavy drops on the floor, it flattens on the bottom, spreads slightly, and then rounds back.
- When a character grabs, presses or leans on something, the clay dents under its hands and keeps the finger marks for a moment before slowly rounding out again.
- When two pieces bump into each other, both wobble.
- Faces are clay too: cheeks squash, mouths stretch wide, heads wobble when the character shakes them.

Every deformation is ELASTIC: the clay squashes, stretches and wobbles, and then always returns to exactly the same shape, the same size and the same colour it had before. The clay never melts, never drips, never tears, never breaks into pieces, never leaves bits behind, and never loses or gains volume: one piece of clay stays one single piece of clay of the same size from the first frame to the last.

Motion is smooth 24fps with real weight, not choppy and not floaty: things feel soft and heavy at the same time.
